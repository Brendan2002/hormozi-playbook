## Recommended skill improvements by section
These are review recommendations, not edits already applied.

### Sections 1–2: routing and venture context
- Check whether leadership-playbook, moozy-growth and moozy-cfo are available before handing work off. If unavailable, explain the limitation and ask for the required facts; do not imply another skill ran.
- Distinguish consumer, retailer and event-organizer offers. A consumer promotion does not automatically improve the store's economics or the organizer's event.
- Keep Etsy, newsletter and pre-launch examples labeled as applications unless a cited source actually discusses that venture. The 10 PDFs do not establish an Etsy-specific or grocery-partnership playbook.

### Section 3: frameworks and economics
- Put the value equation in explicit form: perceived value increases with desired outcome and perceived likelihood, and decreases with delay and effort. Treat it as a conceptual model, not a measured numerical score.
- Put the local-heuristic label immediately beside the market failure threshold, not only later in the source-extension introduction.
- Add a margin and capacity check beside premium pricing. Perceived value does not remove fulfillment costs or the user's approval limits.
- Distinguish 30-day acquisition break-even from a stronger self-funding growth target. The official [Money Models book description](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Alex_Hormozi_100M_Money_Models_How_To_Make_Money?id=xu5_EQAAQBAJ) describes an ideal in which one customer's receipts can support acquiring and serving additional customers within 30 days. Do not relabel basic CAC payback as that full target. Track cash timing, refunds and service costs consistently. Publication listed: August 2025.
- Keep the existing first-avatar analysis; do not claim its customer-selection analysis has been performed for a pre-launch venture.

### Sections 4–5: diagnosis and scorecard
- Make stage placement provisional if headcount and constraints conflict or are unknown. Do not infer the company's stage from street-team size alone.
- Label the /100 audit as the playbook's own rubric. It is not a validated Hormozi scorecard. Use consistent direction: 10 means short delay and low effort, not a larger delay or burden.
- Add diagnostic fields for proof quality, delivery capacity, unit economics, sample-to-purchase conversion, qualified-lead conversion and repeat purchases where relevant. These are proposed operational checks, not claimed verbatim Hormozi checklists.

### Sections 6–7: output and guardrails
- Keep chat-first output, genuine scarcity, honest proof, label-only Moozy health/nutrition claims, and Seth approval for pricing, budgets, guarantees and sponsorship commitments.
- Never turn an AI summary into an official quotation or automatically merge it into the skill.
- Keep fees and renewal terms transparent. A third-party [Discount Plus One-Time Fee transcript](https://spoken.md/episode/23-continuity-offer-discount-one-time-fee-%24100m-lost-1000736805683) contains language about invented fee rationales; this is not a reason to weaken the no-deception guardrail. Secondary transcript, page date November 14, 2025; not checked against audio.

- [Wave's Expanded Employees notes](https://pod.wave.co/podcast/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/24-section-d-expanded-employees-chapter-100m-lost-chapters-audiobook) incorrectly instruct multiplying cost per engaged lead by conversion rate to derive CAC. The correct relationship is cost per engaged lead DIVIDED BY the lead-to-customer conversion rate, with matched cohorts and costs. Example checked: $10 / 0.20 = $50 CAC. Its notes also cite timestamps later than the displayed 25:46 episode duration. The page labels these notes AI-generated; use the original audio for attribution.
