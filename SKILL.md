# Hormozi Playbook

Apply Alex Hormozi's frameworks ($100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models, $100M Scaling Roadmap) to Brendan's real work. Paraphrase the frameworks; never quote the books or guides at length.

## 1. Pick a mode
Choose from the request. Output shapes are in `references/templates.md`. If he shares an existing offer, default to Audit. If nothing exists yet, default to Build.

| Mode | Use when | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Build | New offer, pitch, bundle, lead magnet | Finished offer: name, promise, stack, price, guarantee, scarcity/urgency |
| Audit | Existing page, listing, pitch, price | Scorecard + top 3 fixes + rewritten offer |
| Leads | "How do I get more X" | 30-day lead plan using Core Four |
| Money model | Pricing ladder, upsells, repeat revenue | Attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity map with math |
| Scale diagnosis | "What should I fix next", things feel messy, growth stalled | Stage on the roadmap, its main constraint, top 3 fixes in his scope |
| Quick take | One narrow question | 2-5 sentence answer |

Hand-offs: staffing, delegation or coaching himself as a leader → leadership-playbook. Cost per event/can or ROI → moozy-cfo. Prospect research and pitch drafts → moozy-growth. Hunting new prospects → moozy-opportunity-scout. This skill covers offers, leads, money and scaling priorities.

## 2. Know the venture
Moozy matters most. Read `references/ventures.md` for what's known about each venture (Moozy, DriveOrganized, Close & Open, Harvest blanket) instead of assuming or re-asking. Use TODO fields as the list of what to ask.

If key inputs are missing (audience, price, cost, what's delivered, headcount), ask for all of them in one batched question. If he wants, research them first and label what was researched vs. assumed.

## 3. Frameworks

**Starving crowd (check first).** Rate the market on: pain is real, buyer can pay, buyer is easy to reach, market is growing. If it fails two or more, say so before polishing the offer.

**Value equation.** Value rises with dream outcome and perceived likelihood of getting it; it falls with time delay and effort/sacrifice. Every fix should move one of these four levers. Name the lever.

**Building a Grand Slam offer.**
1. Write the dream outcome in the buyer's words.
2. List every problem or obstacle between them and that outcome (before, during, after).
3. Turn each problem into a solution.
4. Pick delivery vehicles for each solution (done-for-you, done-with-you, do-it-yourself; one-on-one vs. group; formats).
5. Trim the high-cost/low-value items, stack the low-cost/high-value ones.

**Enhancers.**
- Scarcity: real limits on quantity or slots.
- Urgency: real deadlines, seasons, cohorts.
- Bonuses: each solves a named obstacle and has a stated value.
- Guarantees: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee or implied (performance-based). Only offer what can be honored.
- Naming (MAGIC): a magnet reason, the avatar, the goal, a time interval, a container word.

**Pricing.** Price on value, not cost. Premium price funds better delivery, which raises results, which justifies the price. Show the value-to-price ratio.

**Leads.**
- Core Four: warm outreach, posting free content, cold outreach, paid ads.
- Lead getters: referrals, employees, agencies, affiliates/partners.
- Lead magnet: solves one narrow problem for free and points to the paid offer.
- Content: hook, retain, reward.
- Volume (rule of 100; lead magnet types in `references/money-models-leads.md`): commit to a daily number of outreach actions, then scale in order — more of what works, then better, then new channels.

**Money model.** Menus of attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity types are in `references/money-models-leads.md`. Map an attraction offer (gets them in), upsell, downsell (for those who say no) and continuity (recurring). Goal: gross profit from a new customer's first 30 days covers the cost to acquire them. Show the math and label every assumption.

## 4. Scaling Roadmap
Ladder: 0 Improvise · 1 Monetize · 2 Advertise · 3 Stabilize · 4 Prioritize · 5 Productize · 6 Optimize · 7 Categorize · 8 Specialize · 9 Capitalize. Placed mostly by headcount; each stage has one main constraint.

For Scale diagnosis, read `references/scaling-roadmap.md`. Stages 6-8 are verified; Stages 0-5 are unverified, so never fill them in from memory and say so when diagnosing a side venture. Place the business before recommending, advise for its current stage, and frame Moozy company-wide items as recommendations to Seth. Better beats more.

## 5. Audit scorecard
Score 1-10, one line of evidence each. Anchors (3 = weak, 6 = adequate, 9 = strong):

| # | Item | 3 | 6 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market | fails 2+ starving-crowd checks | passes 2-3 | passes all four |
| 2 | Dream outcome | vague or about the product | clear but generic | specific, in buyer's words |
| 3 | Perceived likelihood | no proof | one named result or sample | several specific, verifiable results |
| 4 | Time delay | result in months or unstated | stated, weeks | quick first win stated |
| 5 | Effort/sacrifice | buyer does most of the work | shared | done-for-you or near it |
| 6 | Stack | features listed, no obstacles | some tied to obstacles | every item solves a named obstacle |
| 7 | Enhancers | none | one real one | real scarcity, urgency, bonuses and a guarantee |
| 8 | Name | generic | clear | covers MAGIC elements |
| 9 | Price vs. value | price and value unstated | price stated | value stack vs. price shows a clear ratio |
| 10 | Lead path | no plan | one channel | a daily number plus a second source |

Then: total /100, top 3 fixes ranked by impact ÷ effort (name the lever each moves), and the rewritten offer.

## 6. Output
- Answer in chat. Lead with the offer, score or stage. No preamble.
- Put the finished offer or pitch in one block he can copy.
- Make a file, doc or deck only if he asks.
- Use the matching shape in `references/templates.md`.

## 7. Guardrails
- No fake scarcity, fake deadlines, invented testimonials or fake reviews. Hormozi's frameworks work with real constraints; Etsy and FTC rules also apply.
- Moozy: no health or nutrition claims beyond the label. Discounts, guarantees, pricing, budgets and sponsorship spend need Seth's sign-off — flag this rather than promising it.
- Numbers: show the math, label assumptions, verify arithmetic by running it. Route Moozy unit economics through moozy-cfo.
- Disagree honestly. A weak market, a margin that doesn't work, or advice meant for a bigger stage gets said first, plainly.
