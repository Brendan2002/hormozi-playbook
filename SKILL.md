## 3. Frameworks

**Starving crowd (check first).** Rate the market on: pain is real, buyer can pay, buyer is easy to reach, market is growing. If it fails two or more, flag the weakness before polishing the offer. This two-failure threshold is a local playbook heuristic, not a Hormozi scoring rule. Mark missing evidence unknown rather than counting it as a failure.

**Value equation.** Value rises with dream outcome and perceived likelihood of getting it; it falls with time delay and effort/sacrifice. Conceptually: value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort/sacrifice). This is a reasoning model, not a measured financial formula; do not invent numerical inputs or compute a supposedly objective value score. Every fix should move one of these four levers. Name the lever.

**Building a Grand Slam offer.**
1. Write the dream outcome in the buyer's words.
2. List every problem or obstacle between them and that outcome (before, during, after).
3. Turn each problem into a solution.
4. Pick delivery vehicles for each solution (done-for-you, done-with-you, do-it-yourself; one-on-one vs. group; formats).
5. Trim the high-cost/low-value items, stack the low-cost/high-value ones.

**Enhancers.**
- Scarcity: real limits on quantity or slots.
- Urgency: real deadlines, seasons, cohorts.
- Bonuses: each solves a named obstacle and has a stated value.
- Guarantees: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee or implied (performance-based). Only offer what can be honored.
- Naming (MAGIC): a magnet reason, the avatar, the goal, a time interval, a container word.

**Pricing.** Use customer value to guide positioning, then check costs, margin, capacity and demand before recommending a price. Premium pricing can fund better delivery; it does not guarantee better outcomes. State a value-to-price ratio only when its inputs have credible support; otherwise describe the value qualitatively. Include relevant fulfillment, labor, shipping, platform/payment fees, returns, spoilage and promotional costs. For Moozy, retain section 7 approval requirements.

**Leads.**
- Core Four: warm outreach, posting free content, cold outreach, paid ads.
- Lead getters: referrals, employees, agencies, affiliates/partners.
- Lead magnet: solves one narrow problem for free and points to the paid offer.
- Content: hook, retain, reward.
- Volume (rule of 100; lead magnet types in `references/money-models-leads.md`): commit to a daily number of outreach actions, then scale in order — more of what works, then better, then new channels.

**Money model.** Menus of attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity types are in `references/money-models-leads.md` (unverified; secondary to the books). Map an attraction offer (gets them in), upsell, downsell (for those who say no) and continuity (recurring). Separate two targets: (1) acquisition break-even, where customer gross profit covers CAC; (2) self-funding growth, where the cash available can also acquire and serve additional customers. Do not call the first target proof of the second. The official [Money Models book description, August 2025](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Alex_Hormozi_100M_Money_Models_How_To_Make_Money?id=xu5_EQAAQBAJ) describes an ideal of funding acquisition and service for at least two more customers within 30 days. This is a target, not a promised outcome.

For calculations, match the time window, customer cohort and cost scope. Define CAC = acquisition spend ÷ acquired customers; cost per engaged lead = acquisition spend ÷ engaged leads; CAC = cost per engaged lead ÷ lead-to-customer conversion rate (decimal), not multiplied by that rate. Use consistent denominators and avoid division by zero. Distinguish revenue, gross profit, contribution after acquisition and available cash; reserve for refunds, future delivery and other existing obligations. Define the funding horizon and whether the initial acquisition outlay must be recovered before funding expansion; do not count the same cash twice. State which costs are included, show cash receipt/payment timing, and run the arithmetic. These calculation safeguards are playbook implementation rules.

### Source-backed extensions (checked 2026-11-04)
These additions supplement the frameworks above. Official worksheets are undated unless noted; their tactics remain subject to section 7 and venture-specific economics. Local score thresholds are playbook heuristics, not validated Hormozi scoring systems.

**Niche depth.** After selecting the market, narrow three or four levels by problem, customer and approach; a broad category alone is not an avatar. The worksheet's example moves from relationships to dating, then people over 60, then avoiding awkward first dates. Source: [Pick Your Niche Checklist, p. 1](https://www.acquisition.com/files/pick-your-niche-checklist.pdf).

**Evidence and early progress.** Support perceived likelihood with relevant, genuine case studies; identify how customers will see progress before the final outcome. Examine convenience as well as elapsed time. The worksheet treats price as a quality signal, using wine as its illustration; treat that as a positioning hypothesis, not proof that raising price necessarily improves results. Source: [Pricing & Value Checklist, p. 1](https://www.acquisition.com/files/pricing-value-checklist.pdf).

**Delivery Cube and stricter trimming.** Extend delivery ideation across six dimensions: group size; customer effort; support channel and expertise; consumption format; availability and response speed; and a price-extremes exercise. Ask what delivery would justify ten times the current price, then what could deliver greater value at one-tenth the current price. Include the next problem success creates, not only obstacles to the initial result. Remove low-value components even when inexpensive. Retain only a few costly, high-value components and favor reusable high-value assets elsewhere. A labor-intensive first version can establish demand before investing in scale. Source: [Offer Creation Checklist, pp. 1-3](https://www.acquisition.com/files/offer-creation-checklist.pdf).

**Bonus presentation and partnerships.** Present useful bonuses after stating the price; test adding value before discounting. Give each a benefit-led name, explain its connection to the buyer's problem and the experience it improves, support its usefulness with evidence, and justify any stated monetary value. Prefer tools or checklists when they reduce effort better than extra lessons. Address the customer's next foreseeable problem. Partner benefits can progress from another provider's product to negotiated free units or discounts, commission arrangements, and exclusive features or licenses. Confirm terms before offering them; never invent retail values or partnerships. Source: [Bonus Creation Checklist, pp. 1-2](https://www.acquisition.com/files/bonus-creation-checklist.pdf).

**Guarantee design and stacking.** Tie conditional eligibility to actions genuinely necessary for success. Consider an appropriate remedy: refund, continued or extended service, credit, personal support, reimbursed expenses, release from a commitment, or postponing the next payment until an outcome. The worksheet advocates remedies stronger than a basic refund; evaluate affordability rather than promising them automatically. Performance-based arrangements can use payment per result or a defined revenue/profit share. Stacking options: combine unconditional and conditional protection; cover an initial and larger outcome with separate conditions; or use matching conditions with short and long horizons. State eligibility, measurement, timeframe and remedy clearly as an implementation safeguard. Source: [Unbeatable Guarantee Checklist, pp. 1-2](https://www.acquisition.com/files/unbeatable-guarantee-checklist.pdf).

**Capacity and deadline choices.** Specify whether a real capacity cap applies to the whole business, each week, or each cohort. Urgency can come from a start date, seasonal promotion, expiring price or bonus, or an opportunity that genuinely diminishes over time. Record the actual limit and why it exists before using it in copy. Source: [Scarcity & Urgency Checklist, p. 1](https://www.acquisition.com/files/scarcity-urgency-checklist.pdf).

**Naming and refresh order.** Apply MAGIC to bonuses as well as the main promotion; use shorter timeframes for faster bonus outcomes only when credible. When an offer needs refreshing, try the least operationally disruptive changes first: imagery, ad body copy, headline/name, duration, promotional enhancer, then the sequence and prices of offers. Do not rebuild fulfillment merely because creative has become stale. Source: [Product Naming Checklist, pp. 1-2](https://www.acquisition.com/files/product-naming-checklist.pdf).

**First Avatar / customer-value selection.** Survey existing customers about relevant context, starting position, goals, purchase triggers, referral source and content consumed before buying. Identify the top fifth by spending, retention and fit; find roughly three to five shared qualifiers. Use these to focus messaging, qualification and acquisition channels. Reconstruct their buying journey instead of optimizing only for raw lead count. Gym Launch's example added useful long-form material before sales conversations after finding it common among strong customers. Preserve qualification when removing it increases volume but reduces value. For pre-launch ventures without customer history, label an avatar as a hypothesis rather than claiming this analysis has been performed. Apply business-relevant criteria; do not transplant the chapter's demographic profile to Moozy. Source: [Your First Avatar, printed pp. 2-6, copyright 2021](https://acquisition.com/hubfs/The_Lost_Chapter-Your_First_Avatar.pdf).

**Physical-product value and referral stress test.** Add reusable aids that help customers use the product or solve adjacent problems: Hormozi illustrates supplement bundles with shopping, cooking and budget guides. He also mentions flavor drops as an ecommerce scarcity option. Ask what experience would generate referrals if the business had just one initial buyer and no advertising budget. These are examples, not evidence of a grocery sampling or sponsorship playbook. For DriveOrganized, usage/fit guides are an application hypothesis; for Moozy, any flavor promotion still requires Seth's approval and genuine availability limits. Source: [Capitalism interview transcript, 32:42-36:55, 41:12-44:22](https://www.capitalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Alex-Hormozi-QA-100M-Offers-Building-Product-Brands-You-Can-Sell-1.pdf) (third-party-hosted interview; URL indicates September 2021, exact interview date unverified).

## 5. Audit scorecard
This /100 rubric and impact-versus-effort ranking are local playbook heuristics, not an official or validated Hormozi scorecard. Score 1-10 with one line of evidence each. Anchors per score are in `references/templates.md`. Higher always means stronger: for delay and effort, 10 means faster progress and less customer burden. Mark unsupported items unknown; do not invent scores. If evidence covers only part of a category, describe the observed issue and mark the category unknown unless enough evidence supports an overall rating. Missing reviews alone do not prove all forms of proof are absent.
1. Market (starving crowd)
2. Dream outcome clarity
3. Perceived likelihood (proof, specificity)
4. Time delay
5. Effort/sacrifice
6. Stack quality
7. Enhancers (scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantee)
8. Name
9. Price vs. value
10. Lead path (how buyers find it)

If all ten categories are unknown, say "insufficient evidence to score" and do not report 0/0. When all ten items are supported, total /100. Otherwise show the scored subtotal and available maximum (for example, 42/60 with four unknowns), not a fabricated /100 total. Give the top 3 fixes ranked qualitatively by impact versus effort unless credible numerical estimates exist; name the value lever each moves and provide the rewritten offer.

Before recommending launch or scale, check delivery capacity, proof quality and unit economics. For a lead plan, track activities → engaged leads → qualified leads → purchases → repeat purchases, using defined cohorts and time windows. For sampling, track unique samplers and attributable buyers separately from cans sold; calculate sample-to-purchase conversion only with a valid denominator. These are proposed operational diagnostics, not claimed Hormozi checklists.

## 7. Guardrails
- No fake scarcity, fake deadlines, invented testimonials or fake reviews. Hormozi's frameworks work with real constraints; Etsy and FTC rules also apply.
- Moozy: no health or nutrition claims beyond the label. Discounts, guarantees, pricing, budgets and sponsorship spend need Seth's sign-off — flag this rather than promising it.
- Numbers: show the math, label assumptions, verify arithmetic by running it. Use moozy-cfo for Moozy unit economics when available; otherwise follow the section 1 fallback and never imply it approved the result.
- Sources: prioritize original official material. Mark third-party summaries and transcripts as secondary; verify any claim or formula against the original before presenting it as confirmed Hormozi guidance. Distinguish host comments from Hormozi's statements. If verification is unavailable, label the claim unverified or omit it. Do not promote AI-generated notes into official advice.
- Fees and commitments: disclose mandatory fees, renewal terms, cancellation terms and eligibility clearly. Never invent a fee rationale, reference price, bonus value or expiring deadline. A source recommending questionable tactics does not override these guardrails.
- Disagree honestly. A weak market, a margin that doesn't work, or advice meant for a bigger stage gets said first, plainly.
