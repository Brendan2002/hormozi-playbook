## Money model: pick one from each stage
**Attraction offer** (gets a stranger in, ideally at or near break-even; the goal is cash up front and an easy yes that sets up the upsell). Five types:
- **Win your money back.** The customer puts money down, does prescribed actions or hits a result, and wins it back as cash or store credit. Criteria can be results-based, actions-based or both; make them easy to track, aligned with the customer's goal and useful to the business. Per the book, use it only if your refund rate is already under 5%, otherwise fix the product first. Point the store credit at another, preferably pricier, offer.
- **Giveaway.** One grand prize that your buyers want, a stated value, contact details and a qualifying question as entry, then a compelling discount for everyone who doesn't win. The reading flags giveaways as regulation-heavy and says to consult legal counsel first.
- **Decoy.** Advertise a free or heavily discounted simple offer to attract leads, then present it side by side with a far more valuable premium offer so most pick the premium.
- **Buy X, get Y free.** Frame the deal around how much is free rather than discounting; offer more free than paid (buy 1 get 2 beats buy 2 get 1); the free items needn't match the paid ones.
- **Pay less now or pay more later.** Pay later: card on file, nothing up front, billed only if satisfied, with easy cancellation. Pay now: a meaningful discount (the reading cites 20-50%) plus bonuses. Works best when the result is clear and trackable.

**Upsell** (to current customers, ideally timed right after the first yes). Four types:
- **Classic:** "you can't have X without Y"; solve the new problem the first purchase created.
- **Menu:** present a short menu of options, including a bundle, instead of asking open-endedly; "unselling" some items is part of it.
- **Anchor:** present the premium option first so the main offer looks reasonable.
- **Rollover:** credit what they already paid (or won back) toward a larger purchase, with a deadline so it is now or never.

**Downsell** (for those who say no; tweak the offer, don't just discount). Three types:
- **Payment plan:** change how they pay (the reading suggests splitting into three payments).
- **Trial with penalty:** a free trial conditional on specific actions, with a penalty if they don't do them. Reuse the win-your-money-back criteria.
- **Feature downsell:** lower the price by cutting a feature, so you are not discounting the value.

**Continuity** (ongoing value for ongoing payment; best as the final step after attraction, upsell and downsell). Three types:
- **Continuity bonus:** a valuable bonus for starting the membership today.
- **Continuity discount:** a lower price for committing.
- **Waived fee:** drop a fee for staying.

**Math check.** Gross profit from a new customer's first 30 days vs. cost to acquire them. Show: price, cost to deliver, gross profit, acquisition cost, payback in days. Label every assumption. Run the arithmetic. (Break-even and funding rules are in `SKILL.md`.)

**Guardrails.** Moozy discounts, guarantees and giveaways need Seth's sign-off, and giveaways need legal review. Win-your-money-back and trial-with-penalty terms must be stated clearly up front.

## Leads (UNVERIFIED, from memory; Rule of 100 note is from the Stage 2 guide)
**Rule of 100.** The Acquisition.com Stage 2 guide describes it as spending 100 minutes a day on marketing (Stage01 guide p. 25); other Hormozi material frames it as 100 outreach actions a day, so say which version you mean. Either way, commit to a daily number for long enough to learn what works, then improve in order: more volume, then better, then new channels.

**Lead magnet types** (one narrow problem, solved for free, pointing to the paid offer).
- Reveal a problem they didn't know they had.
- Give one step of a multi-step process.
- A sample (fits Moozy literally).
- A done-for-you slice of the work.
- A diagnostic or scorecard.
- A short trial.

**Content: hook, retain, reward.** Hook in the first line, hold attention with a reason to keep reading, pay off with a useful takeaway.

**Lead getters.** Referrals, employees, agencies, affiliates/partners. For Moozy, store managers and event organizers who refer others count as partners.

**30-day plan skeleton.** Week 1 set the daily number and tracking sheet; week 2 run the best-fit Core Four channel; week 3 review results and cut the weakest; week 4 double the strongest or fix its message. See templates.md for the table.
